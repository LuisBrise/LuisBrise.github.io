I am a physicist and PhD candidate in Condensed Matter and Nanosciences at [UNAM](https://www.posgrado.fisica.unam.mx/), working in the group of Dr. Alejandro Reyes-Coronado. My research sits at the intersection of classical electrodynamics and quantum electron microscopy: I develop semi-analytical and numerical methods to describe how fast electrons exchange linear and angular momentum with nanostructures, and how those interactions show up in electron energy-loss spectra.

## Research Interests

* Theoretical electron microscopy (STEM-EELS)
* Multislice simulation methods
* Nanoplasmonics and electron–matter momentum transfer
* Vibrational and magnetic EELS (magnons)
* High-performance scientific computing

## News

* **2025–2026** — Research stay at Uppsala University (Materials Theory Division), collaborating with [Dr. Jan Rusz](https://www.uu.se/en/contact-and-organisation/staff?query=N7-244) on an efficient magnetic multislice implementation for predicting magnon signals in STEM-EELS.
* **2024** — First-author paper on angular momentum transfer to non-spherical nanoparticles published in *Ultramicroscopy* ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ultramic.2024.114005)).
* **2023–2025** — First-author contributions presented at Microscopy & Microanalysis (M&M) and Frontiers in Optics + Laser Science (FiO), the two leading venues for this work.
* Ongoing — Supervising an undergraduate thesis project at UNAM.

## Background

I completed my B.S. in Physics (2019) and M.S. in Physical Sciences (2023) at UNAM, the latter on [angular momentum transfer from swift electrons to large nanoparticles](https://tesiunam.dgb.unam.mx:443/F/LG9EVDVESL5DPISRX6H5NPCVVA51YMBSBB3MYY94X6HQ8BABTR-36426?func=service&doc_library=TES01&doc_number=000841387&line_number=0001&func_code=WEB-FULL&service_type=MEDIA). My doctoral work extends this to non-spherical geometries and non-local dielectric response, using UNAM's GRID-UNAM supercomputing resources to build efficient computational frameworks for these calculations. Expected defense: 2027.

Through the AALD-PAEP-UNAM research visit grant, I spent time at the Materials Theory Department, Uppsala University, working with Dr. Jan Rusz's group on a magnetic extension of the multislice method (FDES) for simulating magnon-EELS signals — a natural extension of the numerical methods developed during my momentum-transfer work into a new, closely related problem.
